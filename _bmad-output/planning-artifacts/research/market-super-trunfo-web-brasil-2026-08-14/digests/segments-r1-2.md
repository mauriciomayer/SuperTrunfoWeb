## Dimension: Customer segments & behavior (Brasil)

### Findings

- **Claim:** 82.8% of the Brazilian population reported playing digital games in the 2025 Pesquisa Game Brasil (PGB) survey, with Millennials (30–44) the largest generational cohort (49.4%) and women now 53.2% of players.
  - Source: Adrenaline / PSX Brasil / Escola Brasileira de Games, "Número de jogadores no Brasil chega a 82,8% da população, segundo PGB 2025," published ~March 26, 2025, accessed 2026-08-14
  - Confidence: high
  - Class: demographic

- **Claim:** In the newer PGB 2026 wave (7,115 respondents aged 16–55, fielded March 5–13, 2026), overall game-playing participation fell to 75.3% (from 82.8% in 2025), even though 86.7% still rank games among their favorite leisure activities.
  - Source: fmetropolitana.com.br, "Pesquisa mostra que nostalgia impulsiona consumo de jogos digitais no Brasil" (PGB 2026), published August 13, 2026, accessed 2026-08-14
  - Confidence: high
  - Class: demographic

- **Claim:** Nostalgia is an explicit, measured driver of game consumption in Brazil: 62.6% of PGB 2026 respondents play older/classic titles alone, 55.1% play them to have fun with friends; low price/promos (44%) and remakes/remasters with improved graphics (36.3%) are the top stated reasons to revisit old titles.
  - Source: fmetropolitana.com.br, "Pesquisa mostra que nostalgia impulsiona consumo de jogos digitais no Brasil" (PGB 2026), published August 13, 2026, accessed 2026-08-14
  - Confidence: high
  - Class: behavior

- **Claim:** In a broader (non-gaming) nostalgia survey, 83% of Brazilians report an emotional connection to products from the 1980s/90s/2000s, with Gen X anchored to the 1980s and Millennials split between the 1990s/2000s — the exact decades Super Trunfo (launched in Brazil by Grow in the late 1970s, popularized in the 80s/90s) would trade on.
  - Source: E-Commerce Brasil, "O futuro repete o passado: 83% dos brasileiros sentem nostalgia com os anos 80, 90 e 2000," accessed 2026-08-14 (date not confirmed)
  - Confidence: medium (general consumer nostalgia survey, not games-specific)
  - Class: demographic/proxy

- **Claim:** Smartphone is the dominant self-reported gaming platform in Brazil at 40.8% preference (down ~8pp from 2024), ahead of console (24.7%, +3pp) and PC (20.3%, +5.5pp); PGB does not appear to break out "web browser" as its own category, folding it likely into PC.
  - Source: aggregated from Mobiletime.com.br / Gamers & Games / Drops de Jogos coverage of PGB 2025, published March 2025, accessed 2026-08-14
  - Confidence: medium
  - Class: platform-preference

- **Claim:** No Brazil-specific data isolates "web browser" vs. "mobile app" preference for casual/board-style games; closest signal is mobile-first casual play dominating overall (66% of mobile players self-identify as "casual"), skewing 18–35.
  - Source: aggregated Adrenaline / Mobile Gamer BR casual-mobile coverage, 2025, accessed 2026-08-14
  - Confidence: low-medium
  - Class: platform-preference/proxy

- **Claim:** Existing Brazilian digital card-game apps (Truco Brasil, Buraco Brasil, Jogos do Rei, BuracoON, Buraco Jogatina) all ship with both "play with friends" and "play with strangers worldwide" matchmaking as standard — market expects both modes. Truco Brasil reports 4M+ players, Jogos do Rei 3M+ registered users since 2010, BuracoON "hundreds of thousands" MAU.
  - Source: Apple App Store / Google Play listings for Truco Brasil, Buraco Brasil, Jogos do Rei, BuracoON (via MWM, Uptodown, Onrizon product pages), accessed 2026-08-14
  - Confidence: medium (publisher self-reported, but consistent pattern across independent apps)
  - Class: behavior/qualitative

- **Claim:** Pandemic-era: 51.5% of Brazilian players reported more online gaming sessions with friends; more recent PGB data shows 30.5% still scheduling more online social-gaming sessions with friends (up 6.4pp YoY) — social/private-room play is a valued mode, not just matchmaking.
  - Source: Diário do Nordeste / PSX Brasil coverage of PGB, accessed 2026-08-14
  - Confidence: medium
  - Class: behavior

- **Claim:** Grow (original Brazilian licensee of Super Trunfo since the late 1970s) has already released official digital Super Trunfo apps — "Super Trunfo Battle Cards" (multiple decks including a car deck, "Carros") and "Super Trunfo Animais" — showing the brand owner sees digital demand, but no public download/rating/DAU figures were found.
  - Source: Androidlista.com.br / Softonic.com.br / Google Play listing for "Super Trunfo Battle Cards" and "Super Trunfo Animais," accessed 2026-08-14
  - Confidence: low (existence confirmed, no usage metrics obtained)
  - Class: qualitative

- **Claim:** Car-themed digital content has broad appeal in Brazil's mobile market — racing games (Asphalt 8, Real Racing 3, Need for Speed: No Limits, CSR Racing 2) consistently rank among most-downloaded genres; sim-racing described as a fast-growing esport with Brazil as "protagonist" — but this is a proxy for car-content appetite generally, not evidence specific to a car-stats trivia/card format.
  - Source: Adrenaline.com.br / CNN Brasil / Gamers & Games, 2025–2026 articles, accessed 2026-08-14
  - Confidence: low (proxy only)
  - Class: qualitative/proxy

### Leads worth chasing
- Direct PGB report/microsite (pesquisagamebrasil.com.br) likely has raw generational × platform × genre cross-tabs not visible in press summaries.
- App Store/Google Play review mining for "Super Trunfo Battle Cards," "Super Trunfo Animais" was attempted but blocked (page truncated before reviews) — single most direct proxy for who plays Super Trunfo digitally today.
- No survey found asking Brazilians directly "would you play a digital Super Trunfo" or measuring brand recall by age cohort — real gap.
- Ludopedia (Brazilian board-game community) forum threads on Super Trunfo nostalgia exist — could be mined qualitatively, but small self-selected hobbyist community, not representative of mass casual market.

### Searched but not found
- No Super Trunfo-specific demographic/survey data (age breakdown of physical players today, nostalgia-buyer vs. kids-buyer split).
- No Brazil-specific data explicitly comparing web-browser vs. native-app preference for casual/card/board games.
- No independent (non-publisher-reported) download/DAU/MAU figures for Truco Brasil, Buraco Brasil, or Super Trunfo digital apps.
- No academic paper specifically on Brazilian digital board/card game demographics surfaced in this pass.
